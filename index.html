PUBLIC HEALTH × GEOSPATIAL ANALYTICSCASE STUDY / 01

Texas Diabetes
Vulnerability Atlas

Where do diabetes burden and barriers to health overlap? A reproducible, county-level exploration of public data across Texas.

The question

Bring diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, and lack of insurance into one transparent exploratory workflow.

The approach

Python data acquisition and quality checks, FIPS-based joins, percentile-rank scoring, sensitivity analysis, and GIS-ready outputs.

The scope

253 counties analyzed. All 254 county boundaries retained; Loving County estimates are suppressed.

Look inside the analysis

Uses CDC PLACES 2025 release (2023 estimates) and U.S. Census Bureau 2025 cartographic boundaries. Repository outputs identify 110 counties at or above both the diabetes and uninsured medians.

Descriptive and ecological, not a causal model or a clinically validated risk score. Model-based estimates, different age denominators, and equal component weights affect interpretation. Findings shown are from committed repository outputs.

MORTALITY SURVEILLANCE × TREND ANALYSISCASE STUDY / 06

Provisional Drug-Involved Overdose Signals

A CDC/NCHS analysis separating broad national change from drug-specific and HHS-region exceptions.

Key insight: Fentanyl remained the largest tracked category at 36,627 deaths but declined 22.9%; ketamine increased 16.8%, while cocaine rose in Regions 8 and 9 despite a national decline.

Provisional counts, not population-adjusted risk. Drug categories overlap, small baselines magnify percentage change, and results do not establish causation.

HEALTHCARE OPERATIONS × QUALITYCASE STUDY / 05

Emergency Department Throughput Benchmarking

A CMS Care Compare analysis of hospital-reported ED time, volume, state patterns, and public reporting completeness.

Key insight: The median across 4,052 reporting hospitals was 154 minutes; median time rose from 128 minutes in low-volume EDs to 197 minutes in very-high-volume EDs.

Descriptive benchmarking, not a hospital ranking or causal analysis. State values are medians of available hospital medians and are not patient-weighted.

CLINICAL TRIALS × TRANSPARENCYCASE STUDY / 04

Type 2 Diabetes Trial Results Transparency

A reproducible analysis of whether completed Phase 2–4 type 2 diabetes trials have summary results available on ClinicalTrials.gov.

Key insight: 450 of 865 eligible trials (52.0%) had posted summary results. Availability was 58.4% for industry-led trials and 23.9% for non-industry-led trials.

Descriptive registry analysis, not a legal-compliance determination. The sponsor comparison is unadjusted and does not establish causation.

SASPythonClinicalTrials.govQA/QC

Same patients.
Different perspective.

Treatment A shows higher observed success in both stone-size groups. Pool the groups, and the ranking reverses.

Historical, nonrandomized kidney-stone data. A reversal alone cannot determine which estimate answers a causal question. Appropriate adjustment depends on the role of the subgroup variable.
